## Investment committee reasoning
1. The BASE has the highest retrospective holdout return, but is not automatically the winner on the six official Euronext quantitative dimensions.
2. The Ahold + ASML rotation reduces historical volatility and drawdown yet sacrifices 1.227 percentage points of historic holdout return. Other rotations show similar trade-offs.
3. The three geographical rotations require ~EUR 3,208.78 of sells plus ~EUR 3,208.78 of buys (about EUR 6,417.55 gross turnover; roughly 13 theoretical order legs). Execution complexity and delayed NextWise fills matter even when commissions are nil.
4. The 35% Paris-listing ceiling is internal, not a demonstrated contest requirement. Listing country is not the same as economic country-of-risk.
5. Current evidence does not justify a forced rotation, averaging down losers, or deploying the EUR 9,675.76 cash purely to regain recent losses.

## Research limits
- Candidate universe was chosen using information available at 2026-11-18; a retrospective holdout is therefore subject to selection bias and should not be called a fully independent out-of-sample investment performance claim.
- Corporate actions and price history adjustments require confirmation (notably UCB, Ferrari, Dassault Systèmes); Equinor has EUR/NOK FX exposure.
- 8.88% and 9.047% ex-ante volatility estimates arise from different historical pipelines.
- The six official scoring dimensions are known from Euronext materials, but their exact weights and some computational details have not been verified.
- The next national ranking update is at month-end, not available on this decision date.
